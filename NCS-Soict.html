Selection under an uncertain budget

One pass over the items

Items arrive in arbitrary order. Each arrival reveals the item’s mean and variance of resource consumption.

A non-monotone objective

Items have diminishing marginal returns. Adding an item can lower the value of the selected set, as in Max-Cut.

\max_{S\subseteq V} f(S)\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad\Pr\!\left[\sum_{e\in S}W_e\le B\right]\ge 1-\alpha

3
The value oracle is exact in the theorem. The objective is normalized and nonnegative. A Max-Cut example: adding a vertex can turn crossing edges into internal edges. Source: §§1, 3, 5.

The normal distribution

Wₑ ∼ 𝒩(μ, σ²)

Mean μ = E[X]
The long-run average of X and the center of the bell curve.
Variance σ² = E[(X − μ)²]
The expected squared distance from the mean.
Standard deviation σ = √Var(X)
The square root of the variance, in the same units as X.
4
The mean μ is the long-run average and the center of the bell curve. To get the variance, square the distance of X from μ and take its expectation. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance, so it measures spread in the same units as X. The shaded interval contains about 68% of the probability.

The Gaussian chance constraint

Independent Gaussian resources have \mu_e\ge0 and \sigma_e^2>0. Their means and variances add.

g(S)=\mu(S)+z_\alpha\sqrt{v(S)}\le B

Risk level

z_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha) for 0<\alpha<1/2.

Aggregate variance

v(S)=\sum_{e\in S}\sigma_e^2. The square-root term makes the score nonadditive.

5
Use Gaussian closure to explain the exact equivalence. The empty set is feasible. Nonnegative means and variances also imply downward closure. Source: §3.

A family of additive knapsacks

Every tangent-feasible set meets the chance constraint. Each nonempty chance-feasible set belongs to the knapsack at \theta=\sqrt{v(S)}.

\sqrt{x}=\min_{\theta>0}\left(\frac{x}{2\theta}+\frac{\theta}{2}\right)

Cost of item e

c_\theta(e)=\mu_e+\frac{z_\alpha\sigma_e^2}{2\theta}

Capacity

C_\theta=B-\frac{z_\alpha\theta}{2}

6
The minimum is attained at the aggregate standard deviation for positive x. Nonnegative means bound the matching tangent by B/zα. The empty set belongs to every tangent knapsack. Source: Lemma 1.

A geometric grid preserves half the value

By submodularity, one piece has value at least \mathrm{OPT}/2.

\theta_j=\frac{B}{z_\alpha}\left(\frac32\right)^{-j},\qquad j=0,1,2,\ldots

At the grid point below the optimum’s standard deviation

The optimal set has total additive cost at most 9C_{\theta_j}/8.

Two feasible pieces

Split it into two tangent-feasible sets, or separate one chance-feasible singleton from the tangent-feasible remainder.

7
Pick j with θ_j ≤ √v(O) < 3θ_j/2. Explain the two-piece repair and why the singleton safeguard is checked against the original constraint. Source: Lemma 2.

Two candidate sets per active state

For a tangent and an objective guess u, normalize capacity to 1.

  1. Large item. Keep an eligible singleton and terminate the state if its cost is at least ½ and its value density is at least u/4.
  2. Candidate sets. Test the first set, then the second if needed. Add the item only if it fits and its marginal density is at least u/4.
  3. Postprocessing. Run deterministic ½-approximate unconstrained submodular maximization on the first set. Compare that subset, both candidates, and the feasible singleton incumbent.
8
The two sets are disjoint because the second is tested only after the first fails. The postprocessed subset stays feasible by downward closure. For u no greater than the tangent optimum, the fixed state returns value at least u/8. Source: Algorithm 1 and Lemma 3.

Objective guesses activate during the stream

The target guess stays active and follows the same updates as a state started at the beginning, until termination.

For each tangent, track the largest eligible singleton value M_j and density D_j.

u=(1+\delta)^k,\quad k\in\mathbb Z,\quad \frac{M_j}{1+\delta}\le u\le 4D_j,\qquad \delta=16\varepsilon

Before the crossing

Earlier items cannot pass the target state’s density test.

At the crossing

Update the statistics and create the state before testing the arriving item.

9
The target is the largest grid guess no greater than OPTθ. It is above OPTθ/(1+δ), so the lower pruning threshold never removes it. A terminal singleton updates the incumbent. Source: Lemma 4.

Deterministic approximation in one pass

K is the maximum feasible cardinality. The bounds suppress logarithmic factors in the instance widths.

f(S_{\mathrm{alg}})\ge\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{16(1+16\varepsilon)}\ge\left(\frac1{16}-\varepsilon\right)\mathrm{OPT}

Exact chance feasibility for every stream order and 0<\varepsilon<1/16.

Value-oracle queries

\widetilde{O}\!\left(\frac{n+K^2}{\varepsilon}\right)

Memory

\widetilde{O}\!\left(\frac K\varepsilon+K^2\right)

The experiment compares two selection settings

Ten runs per budget at \alpha=0.05. The baseline is the offline heuristic Offline-Greedy-CC.

Email-Eu-core

Influence maximization with the independent-cascade model. The objective is monotone and the oracle estimates spread.

ca-HepTh

Unit-weight Max-Cut on 9,877 vertices and 25,973 edges. The objective is non-monotone and the oracle is exact.

11
Email-Eu-core uses 100 Monte Carlo realizations per oracle query and 1,000 independent realizations for final evaluation. Max-Cut varies ten stream orders on one fixed Gaussian resource instance. Source: §5.

Gaussian feasibility in a single pass

  1. The exact tangent representation turns the Gaussian budget into additive knapsacks.
  2. The geometric grid and online guesses yield a deterministic one-pass approximation.
  3. The memory bound depends on instance widths. Zero-variance items and finite-precision guarantees remain open for this analysis.
